## Pitfalls comparables met

Ask what a store supports: Only OpenDAL and Hadoop have a query. Separate flags do not imply support for their combination; capabilities depend on path or bucket type; flags were missed and fixed piecemeal.

Source: § 4 of the deep research filed as
[`research-operation-catalogue.md`](../../research/research-operation-catalogue.md)
(BK-493, 2026-10-26), whose Sources section dates each citation.

## Findings in the research's results

Q1, an operation no draft entry covered: Absent almost everywhere: OP-18's
capability query (OpenDAL only).

Q3, gating:

| Strategy | Who |
|---|---|
| Declared and checked before dispatch | OpenDAL: `capability()` with about 90 to 100 flags, a pre-dispatch check, and for conditions "never silently ignored" |
| Configured per store, then an error at call time | object_store, which also ignores unsupported tags but errors on unsupported attributes |
| Error at call time only | gocloud, pyarrow, cloudpathlib, smart_open |
| Silent emulation or no-op | fsspec `mkdir` does nothing and `mv` is copy plus delete; cloudpathlib `mkdir` does nothing; pyarrow drops metadata keys; smart_open ignores unknown parameters |
| Path-scoped probe | Hadoop `hasPathCapability`, where False may mean unknown |

OpenDAL gates create_dir, rename, copy, recursive listing, user metadata,
append, presign and the conditional and versioned variants, which matches the
catalogue's optional OP-19 and OP-20. Several gates depend on bucket or
account type and are only found at runtime: Azure rename and recursive delete
need a hierarchical namespace, S3 `RenameObject` needs Express One Zone.
OpenDAL's flags are the effective set, simulations included, and were missed
and fixed piecemeal.

Advisory note 5 (the report's, not a decision): **OP-18:** OpenDAL's effective
flags with a pre-dispatch check, Hadoop's "False means unknown", gates that
depend on bucket type; object_store mixes silently ignoring one option with
erroring on another.

## Clauses retired from spec 053

Spec 053's drafts bound the catalogue's Offer column with two sections. They
left it because their witness needs a call, and OP-18 answers in one place
what a store supports, so its spec binds that column. As drafted:

- **OPS-003, Entries Every Store Offers.** Every store offers every entry whose
  Offer is `v1`.
- **OPS-004, Optional Entries Are Declared.** A store offers an entry whose
  Offer is `optional` only when its backend declares that entry.

with the terms: a store *offers* an entry when it reports the entry as
supported, by OP-18; a backend *declares* an optional entry by the means
OP-18's spec decides.

Source: commit `36f5eab` of PR #1184 (the round-3 dialog in decision log
`sdd/decisions/c886d81c-7d81-58f9-bc3d-f11dc7304e4a.jsonl`; BK-493's dossier,
Status).
