## Pitfalls comparables met

Inspect: "Is it a folder" is ambiguous on object stores and costly to probe. An S3 HEAD on a missing key returns 403 without list permission. mtime is the multipart start time, at about 1 s granularity. The ETag is not a content hash; fsspec offers `ukey` and `checksum` as identity probes.

## Findings in the research's results

§ 1, the comparables: **Comparables (10, the denominator for every "n/10"):**

§ 1, the evidence: Counts are lower bounds: an operation counts only where found

Q2, the cut, inspect (OP-09): The comparables lean to one rich inspect call with
derived probes; Hadoop deprecated single-attribute probes. The identity fields
it returns are opaque tokens, not content hashes.

Q1, an operation no draft entry covered: Integrity and identity tokens
(checksums, unique keys, verified MD5); Verify transfers, detect change without
reading; 6/10.

Q4, decided once for every operation: Identity token: ETag and version are
opaque. An S3 ETag is an MD5 only for a single-part plaintext or SSE-S3 upload;
multipart, SSE-C/KMS and directory buckets give no MD5, and a metadata-only
change leaves the ETag alone.

Advisory note 4 (the report's, not a decision): **OP-09 and OP-14:** ETag and
version are opaque identity tokens; an S3 ETag cannot double as an integrity
check.
