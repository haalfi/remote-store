    Research: Two Kernel Deliveries Measured — Long Loops on Large Diffs¶

    Date: 2026-10-16 Backlog items: BK-389 (PR #1127, with the RFC-0021 detour BK-432, PR #1130) and BK-394 (PR #1144); evidence for BK-431's keep-or-drop decision, closed as keep in the same PR. Status: Measurement complete for the two deliveries. Point-in-time snapshot per sdd/000-process.md § Document types. It follows research-token-spend-means.md and token-usage/report.md, which stay as written; where a figure here re-derives one of theirs, both are given. Related: proposal 6 of research-token-spend-means.md § 6 asked for this measurement; ADR-0045 says a spend figure is judged with the defects shipped.

    TL;DR¶

    Two /ship deliveries on the contract kernel cost 98.25 M units (BK-389) and 57.22 M (BK-394), about $227 and $132, and 72 to 92% of it was the review loop. Neither loop converged: every one of BK-389's 16 rounds and BK-394's 9 posted findings, and both closed on a maintainer's waiver. A round cost 4.5 to 5.6 M units, about twice run B's 2.4 M, on larger diffs and a main context that kept growing; the cost did not scale with the diff's file count or its content (§ 2).

    1. The loop is still where the cost is, and more content makes a round cost more, far less than in proportion: about six times run B's changed lines cost about twice its units per round, because a pass re-reads the PR mostly from cache (§ 2, § 3). The main session carries every fix.
    2. BK-431's smaller reviewer input held in every pass. All 82 passes ran as reviewer and opened at 19.5k to 34.6k tokens, against run B's 42.9k to 43.9k; the prefix fell from 40.6% to 16 to 32% of a pass's context. It did not make passes cheaper, because the PR's own files took the room.
    3. These runs cannot show that run B's saving recurs. Proposal 6 asked for two more /ship deliveries, one of them drawing prose findings only, to decide that. These two have no A run and both drew code findings, and no further delivery is measured.
    4. The largest cut, a three-round cap, trades units for unfixed findings (§ 7): BK-389's rounds 4 onward held 80% of its units and posted 94 of its 120 findings, an upper bound on what a cap would leave unfixed. What a cap would have cost instead is not measured. Reviewing only what changed since the last round targets the findings fixes caused, and a fresh session is not shown to save.

    Context¶

    The delivery measured was /ship BK-389, the RFC-0017 kernel, the next one run on the means of research-token-spend-means.md. The run did not stay one delivery. After twelve review rounds its maintainer paused it for a concept change, RFC-0021 (PR #1130, built and reviewed in its own sessions), then resumed #1127 against it for four more rounds. BK-394, the Memory backend on the kernel, followed as a second /ship with a context prompt in the main checkout. This record measures all three PRs and keeps them apart.

    Sourcing note. Every figure comes from the transcript snapshots an observer session took during the runs, read by token-usage/deliveries.py through tokkit.py's own parser, and from GitHub's review records through scripts/ship_report.py. Citations in square brackets name a field of results/deliveries_bk389_bk394.json as [delivery: field]. Units weight tokens by price, dollars use the fitted prices of report § 1. Which session parts served which PR is a reading of the transcripts, given in § Reproducing.

    • Not split: BK-389's last session also relayed the BUG-326 work in its main context, a few calls left inside BK-389.

    2. The review loops¶

    Neither loop converged: no round of either PR posted zero findings, and both closed on the maintainer's waiver. BK-389 posted 120 findings over 16 rounds and BK-394 60 over 9 [findings_posted, rounds]; a CodeQL submission of 19 refuted findings on #1127 is not counted [bot_submissions_ignored].

    From [rounds, findings_posted, review_loop_rounds_units_m, review_loop_before_first_round_units_m, units_m_per_round, units_m_per_finding]; the main-session row sums rounds[].main_units_m. The rounds and the row before them add up to the review-loop phase of § 1 within 0.01 M. A round runs from the main-session call that spawned its passes up to the call that spawned the next round's, so its cost includes the fix after it; loop cost outside every such span, a session's calls before its first round and a subagent that is no round's pass, joins the latest round spawned before it, so a resume session's opening calls count toward the round they fix. Run B's figures match report.md § Run B; its sixth submission, posted after the run, is counted outside [findings_outside_measured_sessions], as are two findings on #1130 posted from a session outside the delivery.

    • After round 4, only one of BK-389's rounds posted fewer than six. By round: 8, 7, 11, 1, 7, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, then 7 and 7 before the RFC-0021 pause, and 11, 10, 3 and 6 after it [BK-389.rounds[].findings_posted]. 66 of the 120 are tagged loop-introduced, caused by an earlier fix in the same loop (summed from hatch run ship-report 1127's origin column, the CodeQL round left out). The maintainer waived the final round, as the trace records.
    • BK-394 hit the soft ceiling and the divergence check. Its rounds posted 3, 2, 13, 8, 13, 12, 5, 3 and 1 [BK-394.rounds[].findings_posted]; 21 of the 60 are loop-introduced (hatch run ship-report 1144). At round 5 the session asked how to go on past the soft ceiling of five, and at the closing round the divergence check fired on a content guard that round 1 had added and four rounds had patched; it was re-scoped, and the last one-docstring fix was closed by waiver (the PR's decision log).
    • Session breaks reset the main context; nothing else did. BK-389's rounds ran in four sessions, and each started lower than the last one ended: 638k to 378k, 594k to 443k, 559k to 504k [BK-389.rounds[].main_ctx_k]. BK-394 ran all nine rounds in one session, from 91k to 736k. No session compacted except one RFC-0021 session, once.
    • A resume cost one to two rounds. After each of BK-389's three session breaks, the new session's calls before its first round cost 3.56 M, 5.26 M and 7.06 M, and joined rounds 7, 10 and 12, the last round before each break [BK-389.rounds[].opening_units_m]. A round before the breaks cost 4.07 M without them (below). That cost carries the round's fix, which no figure here separates from re-orienting, and the third also holds the last session's re-derivation of the kernel against RFC-0021. Round 12 also took a 3.11 M subagent that was no review pass [BK-389.rounds[].other_units_m].
    • A round costs more on more content, but far less than in proportion. Against run B's 1,452 changed lines and 2.43 M per round: BK-394's 8,479 lines (5.8 times) cost 4.51 M per round (1.9 times); BK-389's rounds 1 to 12, on at most 9,110 lines (6.3 times), cost 4.07 M each (1.7 times) without the 18.99 M of opening and subagent cost that joined rounds 7, 10 and 12; and its rounds 13 to 16, on 18,949 lines (13 times) and with none of that cost, cost 5.30 M each (2.2 times). Doubling BK-389's content raised its cost per round 1.3 times (summed from BK-389.rounds[]'s units_m, opening_units_m and other_units_m; the lines as in § 1). With the opening cost left in, rounds 1 to 12 cost 5.66 M each. RFC-0021's 7,191 lines cost less per round than run B's, 1.90 M, because seven of its 13 rounds ran one narrow pass (below): how a round reads the content counts as much as how much there is. Per finding, BK-389 cost about what run B did (0.74 M against 0.71 M) and BK-394 a little less (0.68 M), though they drew about twice run B's findings per round (7.5 and 6.7 against 3.4).

    RFC-0021's loop was cheap per round because its rounds were narrow. Seven of its 13 rounds ran a single pass, three of them narrow checks of one commit, and its passes cost a median 0.30 M against BK-389's 0.81 M [RFC-0021.rounds, pass_summary]. Its main context still reached 699k [max_main_ctx_k].

    4. Defects¶

    No defect has been reported against either merged PR so far, but each shipped with known open items its own loop filed. "So far" is master's log after #1144, which holds only #1145, and BACKLOG.md, which names neither PR outside the items they filed. The comparison ADR-0045 asks for, the defects a cheaper run ships against those of the run it replaces, has no partner here: neither delivery has an A run.

    • #1144 filed four items at its close (git diff b7deb551c~1 b7deb551c -- sdd/BACKLOG.md): BUG-328, a bytearray write that both Memory drivers refuse and that now answers about the target instead of failing raw; BK-442, remote_store.drivers importing the whole async package, a cost the sync path did not have before; BK-441, a driver that omits a required primitive inheriting an empty body; and BK-440, the Dafny model's write ranking a gate below the order spec 003 states. The first two change behaviour a caller can see. The maintainer chose to file rather than fix BK-441 and BK-442 (the PR's decision log, dialog Finish).
    • #1127 shipped one kernel gap it filed, BK-439: the kernel's flat glob skips the store tree's flat rules. The item is due before the first flat driver with GLOB moves onto the kernel, and none has. Its other new items (git diff 45132599a~1 45132599a -- sdd/BACKLOG.md) are RFC-0021's follow-ups (BK-433, BK-434, BK-436, BK-438), a defect found on master (BUG-323) and two in the review tooling (BUG-324, BUG-325).
    • One defect in the means under test reached master. The reviewer agent's description from #1125 was not valid YAML for a strict parser; Claude Code's loader tolerated it, so every pass here ran, and #1145 fixed it after both deliveries.

    From [wall_min, wait_min, stated_idle_min, active_min].     6. What this changes¶

    BK-431's input is the one control these runs test, and it held; nothing here re-tests the other controls.

    • For BK-431. Every pass opened on the smaller input; the prefix share fell by more than half against run B's 40.6% on BK-389 and BK-394 (16.2% and 17.2%) and by a fifth on RFC-0021 (32.1%) [pass_summary.prefix_pct_mean]; and neither PR edits rvw-pr, so the confound #1125 met does not arise. This supports keeping it. Whether to close it is the maintainer's call, and so is whether spawning every member as reviewer partially supersedes ADR-0036's first decision, which keeps domain personas as a way to staff a scoped lens; this record does not close it.
    • The loop's cost on a large PR is its length, not its passes. The main session took 57% of BK-389's round units and 47% of BK-394's (§ 2's table), and that session carried every fix forward; only a session break lowered its context, and a resume cost one to two rounds. A loop that does not converge pays its context again every round.
    • Proposal 6 ends here unmet. It asked for two more /ship deliveries, one of them drawing prose findings only, to decide whether the saving recurs. These two have no A run to compare with, and both drew code findings; BK-394 also started with a context prompt in the main checkout rather than in a fresh worktree.

    7. Where the cost could be cut¶

    Every cut worth making is in the review loop, and the largest trades units for findings left unfixed. The figures below are what the measured rounds cost, from the result file; what a changed loop would have cost or shipped instead is not measured, and ADR-0045 counts a cut only if the defects shipped do not rise. Run B is the warning: its five rounds shipped two regressions run A's PR had measured and avoided, one of them found in run A's seventh round, though the maintainer judged neither must-fix (research-token-spend-means.md § 5).

    Review only what changed since the last round, keeping one whole-file pass to exit 66 of BK-389's 120 findings and 21 of BK-394's 60 were caused by an earlier fix in the loop (§ 2) A fix that breaks code it did not touch waits for the exit pass ADR-0037's whole-file exit gate stays, or the risk has no bound

    Reproducing¶

    One script writes the result file, from the snapshots and a map of which session parts served which PR. The map is keyed by session ID, so it stays outside the repo with the snapshots, as prep_cost.py's class map does; the result's _provenance block records its SHA-256 and each part's main transcript.

    python sdd/research/token-usage/deliveries.py --repo-root <main checkout> \
    --snapshots <token-kit>/runs --config <token-kit>/deliveries.json \
    --name deliveries_bk389_bk394

    The parts, as read from the transcripts:

    Delivery Parts
    BK-389 the /ship BK-389 build session; three /ship resume 1127 sessions; the first 9 calls of a fourth, before it turned to RFC-0021 at 2026-10-12T15:00Z; the session that resumed #1127 against RFC-0021 and closed it, less its BUG-326 subagent
    RFC-0021 that fourth session from 15:00Z until its resume on 2026-10-15, and the session that continued it from a handoff prompt
    BK-394 the /ship BK-394 build session and its /ship resume 1144
    run B as in report.md § Run B

    tokkit.py gives the same units for each part: report --session <sid> for a whole session, with --until for the cut at 15:00Z, and the excluded subagent's row in subagents --session <sid>. The kit's PR link does not match this split in three places: report --pr 1127 includes the fourth session whole, since it began as /ship resume 1127, and links neither the RFC-0021 continuation nor #1127's last session, whose first prompt is a handoff. The kit reads Claude Code's transcript folders, not a snapshot folder, so a re-run of tokkit.py on these snapshots needs them laid out as a projects folder; deliveries.py reads the snapshot folder directly.
