    Research: What each backend's authority allows in a path or key¶

    Date: 2026-10-21 Status: Research complete — a reference table, with dated measurements (§ 5)

    1. Purpose¶

    Every storage backend has its own rules for what a path or key may be. Those rules live in different authorities: an RFC, the POSIX standard, vendor documentation, an expired Internet-Draft. They are stated in different units (bytes, UTF-16 units, characters, segment counts), and they are often recalled from memory rather than read.

    This document is a reference: for each externally governed backend, what its own authoritative source permits in a path or key, with the source cited. § 2 to § 4 record the external authorities, with labelled measurements where no authority speaks: § 2's cells marked "measured", § 3.5's CPython runs, § 3.7's SDK URL capture and § 4's references to § 5. § 5 holds the dated measurements of what services and local implementations did, labelled as measurements and not authorities. The document decides nothing and states nothing about what remote-store accepts.

    Scope. The externally governed backends of remote-store v0.33.0 (FEATURES.md § Backends): local, s3, s3-pyarrow, azure, sftp, graph, http. memory, sql-blob and sql-query have no external authority; their key rules are remote-store-defined.

    Derivation. Every claim about an authority comes from a primary source, fetched on 2026-10-21 (WebFetch, or Python urllib / pdftotext for POSIX, Apple and Python pages) unless its subsection dates a later search; of the five sources the quote check added below, four were fetched on 2026-10-22 (the rfc6874bis datatracker page in § 3.1; the Microsoft Q&A answer, the azure-sdk-for-python pull request and the Azurite middleware in § 3.7) and one on 2026-10-23 (Linux limits.h in § 3.2). Every double-quoted passage in § 3, of any length, was then checked against the raw text of a source its subsection lists by bk-484-key-model/probes/check_quotes.py: 92 of 92 found on 2026-10-23. A passage counts as found when it occurs in one source as a substring, or, for a passage elided with …, when its fragments occur as substrings in the passage's order, each after the previous one ends (pinned by probes/test_check_quotes.py). Both after the script's normalisation, which decodes HTML entities, collapses whitespace, folds quote marks, dashes and non-breaking spaces, and deletes backticks, * and zero-width characters (its docstring lists them); a very short quote can match by chance. The check corrected three quotes, narrowed one date and added the five sources it found missing. Points that no primary source settles after two rounds of search are marked "Not documented" and listed in § 4. Statements from SDK source, SDK issue trackers, Microsoft Q&A answers or emulators, and measurements, are labelled as such.

    3. Survey: the authority per backend¶

    3.1 URI layer: RFC 3986, RFC 8089, RFC 9110 / 9112¶

    Applies to http and to Graph request URLs.

    • Grammar (RFC 3986 §3.3, §2.1–§2.3): segment = *pchar, pchar = unreserved / pct-encoded / sub-delims / ":" / "@". "Aside from dot-segments in hierarchical paths, a path segment is considered opaque by the generic syntax."
    • Empty segments are grammatical (segment may be empty). §3.3: without an authority, "the path cannot begin with two slash characters".
    • Dot segments are removed by remove_dot_segments (§5.2.4). /../g resolves to http://a/g (§5.4.2), so .. above root is dropped silently, not an error.
    • Length: no path, segment or URI limit appears in RFC 3986 (negative finding from a full-text search; the only figure is 255 for host names, §3.2.2). RFC 9110 §4.1: "It is RECOMMENDED that all senders and recipients support, at a minimum, URIs with lengths of 8000 octets". RFC 9112 §3 gives the same floor for request lines and the 414 obligation ("MUST respond with a 414"). RFC 9110 §15.5.15 only defines the status.
    • Case: scheme and host are case-insensitive, other components case-sensitive (§6.2.2.1).
    • Encoding equivalence: "URIs that differ in the replacement of a reserved character with its corresponding percent-encoded octet are not equivalent" (§2.2). So a%2Fb is one segment and a/b two. Unreserved octets may be decoded at any time (§2.4, §6.2.2.2). Since . is unreserved, %2E%2E normalises to .. (inference from those two clauses).
    • Server namespace: "Each origin defines its own namespace" (RFC 9110 §4.3.1). Beyond URI syntax, the HTTP server's path rules are opaque.
    • RFC 8089 (file:): §2 ABNF; system-specific operations in Appendix D (D.1 POSIX, D.2 DOS/Windows, D.3 Mac OS X); drive letters and UNC as nonstandard variants in Appendix E (E.2, E.3); collected nonstandard ABNF in Appendix F. §4: names "SHOULD be encoded as octets according to the UTF-8 character encoding scheme".
    • Status: RFC 3986 is STD 66, updated by RFC 7320 and RFC 8820, not obsoleted. Datatracker searches for 3986bis, 3986 and uri (active and expired drafts) found no revision of the generic syntax. The nearest attempt, draft-ietf-6man-rfc6874bis (IPv6 zone IDs only), is "Expired (IESG: Dead)"; its latest revision is dated 2023-07-02.

    3.3 macOS¶

    • APFS (FAQ): "APFS accepts only valid UTF-8 encoded filenames for creation, and preserves both case and normalization of the filename on disk in all variants." Case-insensitive is the macOS default. Since High Sierra, APFS "is normalization-insensitive in both the case-insensitive and case-sensitive variants". It rejects code points unassigned in Unicode 9.0. The early iOS 10.3 / Sierra-preview case-sensitive APFS was normalisation-sensitive.
    • APFS on disk (Reference PDF, rev. 2020-06-22): names are null-terminated UTF-8; the length field is 10 bits; the lookup hash uses NFD then CRC-32C. No 255 limit is stated.
    • xnu syslimits.h: NAME_MAX 255 ("max bytes in a file name") with the note "Many filesystems (including HFS & APFS) may support names longer than NAME_MAX bytes", and PATH_MAX 1024.
    • HFS+ (TN1150): "up to 255 Unicode characters", typed HFSUniStr255 of 16-bit units. "HFS Plus stores strings fully decomposed and in canonical order", compares case-insensitively; HFSX is case-sensitive. U+2000–U+2FFF are not fully decomposed, so it is an NFD variant, not exact NFD.
    • : ↔ / mapping: no Apple documentation page states it (File System Programming Guide and TN1150 are silent). Apple's own source does: xnu bsd/vfs/vfs_utfconv.c, "In Mac OS X the slash is illegal in a file name. So for us the colon is a slash and a slash is a colon"; HFS core/hfs_catalog.c passes ':' as the alternate slash. An Apple DTS engineer on the developer forums: a file created as Hello/Cruel/World!.txt through the Carbon API lists as Hello:Cruel:World!.txt in ls. So : is a legal POSIX-layer character that the Finder shows as /.

    3.4 Windows¶

    • Reserved characters (Naming Files): < > : " / \ | ? *, NUL, and 1–31 "except for alternate data streams".
    • Reserved names: "CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, COM1 … COM9, COM¹, COM², COM³, LPT1 … LPT9, LPT¹, LPT², and LPT³". With any extension too: "NUL.txt and NUL.tar.gz are both equivalent to NUL". COM0 and LPT0 are not on the list (OneDrive's list does include them, see § 3.9).
    • Trailing space or period: "Do not end a file or directory name with a space or a period", framed as a shell/UI limitation. In practice Win32 normalisation (.NET "File path formats" § Path normalization) removes the period from a segment that ends in a single period (a segment of three or more periods is a valid name) and, when the path has no trailing separator, all trailing periods and spaces from the last segment. A name like hidden. is reachable only through \\?\.
    • Separators: / is converted to \ except under \\?\; "a series of slashes that follow the first two slashes are collapsed into a single slash".
    • Dot segments: "Parent directories are only removed if they aren't past the root of the path." \\?\ disables all parsing.
    • Length (Maximum Path Length Limitation): MAX_PATH 260 including drive and NUL; extended paths about 32 767 ("approximate"); components "commonly 255", really the volume's lpMaximumComponentLength. The filesystem comparison page gives 32 760 and 255 "Unicode characters" for NTFS, exFAT and FAT32; the ReFS overview gives 255 "Unicode characters" per name and "32K Unicode characters" per path, the same as NTFS. Removing MAX_PATH needs both LongPathsEnabled = 1 and the longPathAware manifest element. Directory creation without opt-in is limited to MAX_PATH − 12.
    • Case: case-insensitive and case-preserving. Per-directory case sensitivity via fsutil file setCaseSensitiveInfo since Windows 10 build 17107, NTFS only, on empty directories.
    • Encoding: "the file system treats path and file names as an opaque sequence of WCHARs", with no Unicode normalisation.
    • Python: os.path.isreserved (Windows, 3.13+) approximates these rules and is deliberately over-inclusive (adds CONIN$, CONOUT$). PurePath.is_reserved() was removed in 3.15.

    3.5 Python: pathlib, os.path and filename decoding¶

    Python is not a backend, but its path model is the one developers bring to a key. pathlib and os.path normalise lexically, without touching a filesystem, and they disagree with each other and with the backends above. Docs as of Python 3.15.1.

    Pure paths do no I/O. "Pure path objects provide path-handling operations which don't actually access a filesystem." PurePosixPath and PureWindowsPath can be instantiated on any system.

    Construction (PurePath(*pathsegments)): "Spurious slashes and single dots are collapsed, but double dots ('..') and leading double slashes ('//') are not, since this would change the meaning of a path for various reasons (e.g. symbolic links, UNC paths)". More than two leading slashes collapse to one (PurePosixPath('///etc').root is '/'). Trailing slashes are dropped: "pathlib normalizes Path("my_folder/") to Path("my_folder")". An empty path is .: "When pathsegments is empty or consists only of empty strings, the current directory is assumed". "If a segment is an absolute path, all previous segments are ignored". .. is removed only by Path.resolve() ("this is the only method to do so"), which touches the filesystem.

    os.path.normpath goes further: it collapses "redundant separators and up-level references so that A//B, A/B/, A/./B and A/foo/../B all become A/B. This string manipulation may change the meaning of a path that contains symbolic links." On Windows it also converts / to \.

    Input PurePosixPath os.path.normpath (POSIX) Same key on S3 / Azure flat?
    a//b a/b a/b Distinct (§ 3.6, § 3.7)
    a/./b a/b a/b Distinct, if stored literally
    a/../b a/../b b Distinct; S3 checks the .. count rule
    a/ a a Distinct; a/ is an S3 folder marker
    //a //a //a Distinct
    `` (empty) . . No key (S3 minimum length 1)

    The two Python columns were run on CPython 3.14.8 (PurePosixPath(s) and posixpath.normpath(s) for each input) and match the 3.15 docs. The last column is the gap: inputs pathlib treats as equal can be distinct keys on a flat store.

    Case follows the flavour: PurePosixPath('foo') == PurePosixPath('FOO') is False, PureWindowsPath('foo') == PureWindowsPath('FOO') is True. Matching (full_match, glob) follows the same platform default, overridable with case_sensitive= (3.12+). os.path.normcase lowercases on Windows and returns the path unchanged elsewhere. No case or Unicode normalisation happens on POSIX, and the pathlib docs never mention Unicode normalisation.

    Windows flavour: UNC shares count as drives (PureWindowsPath('//host/share/foo.txt').drive is \\host\share), a rooted segment such as \foo keeps the current drive, and / is accepted on input and rendered as \. Reserved-name checks moved to os.path.isreserved (§ 3.4).

    URIs: Path.as_uri() and Path.from_uri() produce and parse file: URIs "conforming to RFC 8089", absolute paths only. "File URIs are not portable across machines with different filesystem encodings."

    Filename decoding. Paths are str. On POSIX, Python decodes filenames with the filesystem encoding and surrogateescape: undecodable bytes ≥ 128 become lone surrogates U+DC80–U+DCFF and round-trip on encode (PEP 383; os docs). Windows uses surrogatepass. UTF-8 mode is the default from Python 3.15 (PEP 686); on macOS the filesystem encoding is UTF-8 regardless. So a surrogate-escaped str round-trips on Linux, while APFS refuses to create the non-UTF-8 name (follows from the APFS FAQ; not run). bytes(path) gives the os.fsencode() form, "only recommended under Unix"; pathlib, unlike os, accepts no bytes paths.

    3.6 Amazon S3¶

    General-purpose buckets ("Naming Amazon S3 objects"):

    • "a sequence of Unicode characters encoded in UTF-8, with a maximum length of 1,024 bytes"; prefix and delimiter count toward it. PutObject: minimum length 1. "Object key names are case sensitive."
    • "The Amazon S3 data model is a flat structure … There is no hierarchy of subbuckets or subfolders." Console folders are zero-byte prefix/ objects.
    • "You can use any UTF-8 character in an object key name." The page then grades characters: safe (0-9 a-z A-Z ! - _ . * ' ( )), might require special handling (& $ @ = ; / : + , ?, space, 0x00–0x1F, 0x7F), avoid (\ { } ^ % \ [ ] " < > ~ # |`, bytes 128–255).
    • Hard .. rule, all access paths: keys with ../ "are valid if, when parsed left-to-right, the cumulative count of relative path segments never exceeds the number of non-relative path elements encountered". videos/2014/../../video1.wmv is valid, videos/../../video1.wmv is not.
    • Period-only segments (., ..): avoid; tools may resolve them.
    • Console-only limits: keys with a ./ prefix and keys ending in / cannot be uploaded through the console (REST, CLI and SDK can); console download strips trailing periods.
    • XML: ListObjectsV2 cannot carry characters such as 0–10 in XML 1.0; encoding-type=url encodes Delimiter, Prefix, Key, StartAfter. In XML request bodies CR/CRLF normalise to LF unless entity-encoded.
    • Keys sort "lexicographically by their UTF-8 encoded byte values".

    Directory buckets (S3 Express One Zone):

    • "organize data hierarchically into directories". "The only supported delimiter is a forward slash (/)." Directories are created with the first key and empty ones are deleted recursively on object delete.
    • ListObjectsV2: prefixes must end in /; results unsorted; no StartAfter.
    • DeleteObjects keys "must contain at least one non-white space character" and "cannot contain Unicode control characters, except for" \n \t \r.
    • RenameObject "will not succeed on objects that end with the slash (/)".
    • "Directories are created during PutObject or CreateMultiPartUpload operations and automatically removed when they become empty"; "There is no upper limit to the number of objects and subdirectories in a directory" (performance design patterns page). No maximum depth or per-segment length is documented.
    • Not documented by AWS (second search, 2026-10-21, covering the directory-bucket user-guide pages, the PutObject, ListObjectsV2, DeleteObjects and RenameObject API pages, and web searches): // or empty segments; . or .. segments, including whether the general-purpose .. counting rule applies; PutObject of a key ending in /; leading /. These are settled only by a live probe against a directory bucket.

    // in general-purpose buckets: no AWS document states it. AWS SDK issue reports (aws/aws-cli#6253, aws/aws-sdk-go#2559, not AWS-staff statements) show A and //A as distinct objects, and SDK-side URI cleaning as the cause of 404s on such keys. Indicative only.

    3.7 Azure Blob Storage and ADLS Gen2¶

    "Naming and Referencing Containers, Blobs, and Metadata" (updated 2026-07-21):

    • Violations fail with 400 Bad Request. Some characters "are not valid in URL paths even when encoded" and may fail.
    • Blob name: "any combination of characters", 1–1024 characters (legacy emulator: 256), case-sensitive, reserved URL characters escaped. Control characters 0x00–0x1F and invalid code points "are also not allowed".
    • Segments: flat accounts ≤ 254; HNS accounts ≤ 63 "including path segments for account name and container name".
    • Advisory: "Avoid blob names that end with a dot (.), a forward slash (/), a backslash (), or a sequence or combination of the two. No path segments should end with a dot (.)." Same note for HNS directory names. Not a rejection.
    • Not recommended: U+0080–U+009F (with gaps), U+FDD1–U+FDEF, U+FFF0–U+FFFF, U+nFFFE/nFFFF: they "may fail UTF-8 or MBCS decoding".
    • Flat by default: a delimiter creates a virtual hierarchy; /a, /a.txt, /a/b, /a/b.txt coexist.
    • HNS: real directories; directory names 1–1024 characters, case-sensitive. / is the only supported delimiter (Data Lake known issues). HNS cannot be reverted to flat. "Path - Create" gives no path constraint beyond the InvalidResourceName and PathConflict errors.
    • Container / filesystem names (the source of the often-misattributed "63"): 3–63 characters, lowercase letters, digits, -, no --; ADLS also allows a leading $ (for $root).
    • HNS lengths: the only documented limits are 1–1024 characters per directory name and the 63-segment cap; a Microsoft Q&A answer by a -MSFT handle confirms "the max depth of directories are 63 (including account name and container name) not 254". No per-segment 255 limit exists in ADLS docs; that figure belongs to Azure Files: "Individual components in the path can be a maximum of 255 characters in length" (Naming and Referencing Shares, Directories, Files, and Metadata).
    • Trailing dots, Azure Files only: the Files naming page states that by default "dot (.) characters at the end of directory and file names in request URLs are ignored or left out", unless the x-ms-allow-trailing-dot header is set (version 2022-11-02 on). No Blob or Data Lake page states the same for blob names; a search on 2026-10-22 found none. § 5 measured it on an HNS account.
    • Backslash: no learn.microsoft.com page states a conversion. A merged Microsoft PR in azure-sdk-for-python (#48883, 2026-09-04) states "The service canonicalizes \ to / when it rebuilds the signed resource to validate the signature"; the .NET SDK's BlobSasBuilder.GetCanonicalName does blobName.Replace("\\", "/"). That is signature validation; whether the stored name changes is not stated.
    • Client side, Python SDK (azure-storage-blob 12.31.0, azure-storage-file-datalake 12.26.0, requests 2.34.2, urllib3 2.8.0; URL captured by patching requests.Session.send, no network):
      • The SDK quotes names with quote(name, safe='~/') and does not normalise, but urllib3's parse_url removes dot segments before sending: a/./b → /c/a/b, a/../b → /c/b, and x/../../b → /b, a request outside the container, for both blob and datalake clients.
      • %2E is no workaround: the SDK re-quotes it to %252E, storing the literal text %2E.
      • a//b is sent unchanged. The datalake path client strips leading and trailing / (path_name.strip("/")); BlobClient does not.
      • \ is sent as %5C, unconverted.
    • Not documented by Microsoft (second search, 2026-10-21): how the service treats raw ., .., // or \ in a name on either endpoint. Azurite (non-authoritative) stores // literally and converts \ to / with the comment "Azure Storage Server will replace '\' with '/' in the blob names" (its quote marks rendered as ' here). Settled only by a live probe that bypasses client normalisation (for example curl --path-as-is). § 5 records such a probe against an HNS account. For a flat account it records a measurement through the SDK only, which leaves raw ., .. and \ unmeasured.

    3.8 SFTP¶

    • What runs in practice: OpenSSH implements protocol version 3, defined in draft-ietf-secsh-filexfer-02 (PROTOCOL §4; openssh.org specs page).
    • §6.2 File Names (-02): "This protocol represents file names as strings." "File names are assumed to use the slash ('/') character as a directory separator." Absolute names start at the filesystem root, others are relative to the user's home. "Servers SHOULD interpret a path name component '..' as referring to the parent directory, and '.' as referring to the current directory." "An empty path name is valid, and it refers to the user's default directory". "Otherwise, no syntax is defined for file names by this specification."
    • Encoding: none specified for names in v3 (UTF-8 appears only for status messages). UTF-8 filenames come with v6 (draft -13: "A server that does not send this extension MUST send all filenames encoded in UTF-8"), which OpenSSH does not implement.
    • REALPATH (§6.11) canonicalises to an absolute path; the canonical form is server-defined.
    • OpenSSH extensions: posix-rename@openssh.com (v3 RENAME is not POSIX rename), hardlink@openssh.com, expand-path@openssh.com (tilde expansion), home-directory, limits@openssh.com. SSH_FXP_SYMLINK arguments are reversed relative to the draft and that order was kept.
    • IETF status: the SSHM working group charter includes documenting SFTP "as they are currently implemented", but there is no adopted document. draft-ietf-sshm-filexfer does not exist. The only candidate is the expired individual draft draft-spaghetti-sshm-filexfer-00 (2025-07-01), still v3, still "no syntax is defined for file names", adding that servers must stop .. escaping access limits.
    • Consequence: the effective rules are the server's filesystem (usually POSIX, see § 3.2), reached through opaque strings.

    3.9 Microsoft Graph (OneDrive, SharePoint)¶

    • Invalid characters (Restrictions and limitations): " * : < > ? / \ |; "You can't use leading and trailing spaces in file or folder names." # and % are unsupported in some organisations until an admin enables them.
    • Invalid names: ".lock, CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, COM0 - COM9, LPT0 - LPT9, vti, desktop.ini, any filename starting with ~$"; "vti" is banned anywhere in a name. A folder name cannot start with ゛ or ဧ. Whether a reserved name with an extension (CON.txt) is blocked is not documented by Microsoft (second search, including KB 2933738, which covers only the legacy groove.exe client); needs a test upload.
    • Path length: decoded. SharePoint limits service description (2026-10-06): "The entire decoded file path, including the file name, can't contain more than 400 characters … The limit applies to the combination of the folder path and file name after decoding." A Microsoft Q&A staff answer (2024-08-01) agrees: "the path length of a file is calculated based on decoding." The count is over the server-relative path ("The server relative path to a file or folder has a total maximum of 400 characters", migration assessment docs). One support article contradicts this on the same page: its table says "decoded", its how-to section says "special characters are URL-encoded and count toward the 400-character limit".
    • Case: "File system path identifiers used with colons … and filenames specified in the URL … are not case-sensitive"; ":/Folder-A/Foo.txt is treated the same as :/folder-a/foo.TXT". Names are stored and returned as given. That a.txt and A.txt cannot coexist in one folder follows from case-insensitive lookup; no page states it outright.
    • URL length: no general Graph limit is documented. Two pages give 2,048 characters in narrow contexts: Graph known issues, for $filter with advanced query capabilities; and the OneDrive file-handlers page (ms.date 2017-09-10), on activation URLs, "less than the maximum URL length of 2048 characters", without saying which component imposes that maximum.

    • S3 directory buckets: //, empty, ., .. segments; PUT of a key ending in /; leading /. AWS documents none of these; needs a live probe. § 5's probe covers them, but its 2026-10-22 run could not create a bucket.
    • S3 general-purpose: // stored literally (indicative SDK issue reports only). § 5's probe covers it; the same run was refused at authentication.
    • Azure flat namespace: service treatment of raw ., .. and \. Microsoft documents none; needs a live probe with an unnormalised request path. Through the SDK, //, a leading / and a trailing / were stored as sent (§ 5). The HNS endpoint is measured in § 5; Azurite, a flat emulator, is not an authority for the service.
    • Graph: whether a reserved name with an extension (CON.txt) is blocked. Microsoft documents it nowhere; needs a test upload.
    • Graph: that a.txt and A.txt cannot coexist is inferred from case-insensitive lookup, not stated.
    • Graph: ., .. and empty segments in a path, and a trailing /. No Microsoft page cited in § 3.9 states them, and a web search on 2026-10-22 found none; needs a live probe.

    5. Measured: raw keys against a service and local implementations (2026-10-22)¶

    This section is not an authority. It records what one Azure account and three local implementations did with eleven raw keys, and what a second, flat Azure account did with keys sent through the SDK.

    Method. The script is bk-484-key-model/probes/probe_paths.py, and its output is results-2026-10-22.json, which carries no account, resource, region or zone name and no exception message text. The OpenSSH column was re-run on 2026-10-23, after the last row's key was corrected to climb above the chroot root (see the note under the table).

    • For each key, the script sends a PUT byte for byte through http.client, so no SDK or urllib3 step rewrites the path (§ 3.7).
    • It then lists the target and sends a GET on the same raw path.
    • Azure requests use a container SAS and x-ms-version: 2023-11-03. S3 requests are signed with botocore's S3SigV4Auth, which leaves the path as is.
    • SFTP goes through paramiko, which sends path strings unchanged. The directories a, e and g are created first, because POSIX resolution is physical.
    • Each target gets a fresh bucket, container or folder, deleted afterwards.

    Targets.

    • An Azure storage account with HNS (is_hns_enabled true), through the blob endpoint. The DFS endpoint was not probed.
    • Azurite 3.35.0, a flat emulator.
    • MinIO, Chainguard image latest-dev, unpinned.
    • OpenSSH in atmoz/sftp:alpine, chrooted with home /.

    AWS was not measured. The general-purpose bucket's CreateBucket was refused with InvalidAccessKeyId (the stored key no longer exists). The directory bucket's CreateBucket was refused in each of the six zone IDs the script tries, with the same key. The console showed AccessDenied for each, but the script of that run dropped the codes before writing the results file, which records only that no directory bucket was created; the script now keeps one code per zone (attempt_codes). Whether the account's policy would allow a directory bucket is not known.

    p/... PUT 400 InvalidUri; a GET on the path answers 200 as sent as sent as sent

    OpenSSH names are relative to the probe folder, which is two segments below the chroot root. The last row climbs one level above the root on every target: OpenSSH was sent <folder>/../../../x, one .. more than the others. Both runs of each local target gave the same results.

    Readings.

    • The Azure HNS service rewrites a raw path. It collapses empty segments, drops a leading /, removes ., resolves .., converts \ to / and strips a trailing .. Each rewritten key was stored under a name other than the one sent, with no error. Microsoft documents the trailing-dot rule only for Azure Files (§ 3.7). The p/... row fits dot-stripping leaving an empty name, and the 200 on its GET fits that GET reaching the directory p. Both are inferences; neither was probed separately.
    • On Azure, .. resolves before authorisation. The 403 means the resolved path lay outside the container the SAS covers. Under a credential that covers the whole account (an account key or an account SAS), the same request would be authorised against the resolved path. That is an inference; it was not run.
    • Azurite differs from the service on every row except the control and \. It stores the other keys as sent.
    • MinIO refuses empty, . and .. segments and drops a leading /. What AWS does with these remains open (§ 4).
    • OpenSSH resolves paths in the server's filesystem. The write one level above the chroot root was refused with EACCES, and the GET that followed answered ENOENT. That fits /.. resolving to / (§ 3.2), where the root-owned chroot root refuses the write, but this probe cannot tell it apart from a refusal of the escape itself.

    A flat account, through the SDK (2026-10-22). The maintainer measured a flat-namespace account separately and posted the output on PR #1173: azure-storage-blob 12.31.0, Entra sign-in, blobs under a random prefix in an existing container. The account's HNS flag was not read (get_account_information answered 403), so its flat namespace rests on the repo's storage configuration and fits the results; it was not checked.

    • m//b and q// were stored as sent, so an empty segment is a level of its own.
    • A leading / was kept.
    • m/ was stored as a blob of its own, and m did not exist.
    • r/./s was stored as r/s and r/../t as t. urllib3 removes dot segments before sending (§ 3.7), so this run does not show what the service does with them.
    • \ and a trailing . were not sent.

    The SDK sends // unchanged and BlobClient strips no / (§ 3.7), so the first three keys reached the service as written. That is an inference from the client capture, not a raw probe.
