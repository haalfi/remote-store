    RFC-0024: A spec-first reset toward v1¶

    Status¶

    Accepted, 2026-10-23, by the maintainer. Tracked as BK-485, § 9 steps 1 and 2 of research-spec-first-reset.md. That research doc is the decision record for D1 to D11, with the options each rejected; this RFC makes them binding process, records step 1, and answers the research's open points 5, 9 and 10. Decisions taken in this RFC's own session: decision log sdd/decisions/69e4bf99-0899-474b-bb47-606425c66a1a.jsonl.

    Amended by RFC-0025, accepted 2026-10-25: R1's D2, D8, D9 and D10 rows, R2, R3's classes K and P, R4, R5 and § Impact's Process bullet hold as RFC-0025 amends them while master's code is 0.33.

    It changes no spec format. SPEC-DESIGN.md and ADR-0048 stay the authority for what a spec states and the form it takes, with one bounded exception in § R4. The format RFC (§ 9 step 6) amends them with the research's F1 to F6.

    Summary¶

    remote-store v1 is specified first, as what a caller can observe at the Store boundary, and implemented only after. The spec work runs on master, which publishes nothing until v1 is implemented and ships it as one 1.0.0. release/0.33.x carries fixes to the 0.33 line in the meantime. RFC-0017's migration onto the kernel is paused from step 2 on, until D10 decides when kernel and driver work resumes. A difference between a v1 spec and 0.33 is a break record or a 0.33 defect, never a defect in the v1 spec.

    Motivation¶

    The research's § 1 is the motivation: specs 001 to 050 mix what a caller observes with how 0.33 achieves it, and RFC-0017's D3 steps build the kernel against them. Recorded decisions bind no one until a process document carries them. Three things that follow from them also had no answer:

    • What the pause does to the work queued behind D3. Open items name a D3 step as their due date or as their fix's home (§ R3 lists them).
    • How a break reaches a user. D1 promises a break record and a migration note, and the research did not say what a break record is (open point 9).
    • What the authority rules say when a v1 spec disagrees with 0.33 code. 000-process.md Rule 3 makes the code wrong, which for a spec designed ahead of its implementation would be true of every clause that changes behaviour.

    Proposal¶

    R1. D1 to D11 are binding¶

    Each row is the binding rule; the decision, and the alternative it rejected, is the research's § 2 row of the same number.

    D Binding rule Carried out by
    D1 The v1 Store API is specified as consistent observable semantics. Behaviour may differ from 0.33; every difference is a break record (§ R5) before it is implemented and a migration-guide section when it is. every flow; § R5
    D2 Spec work runs on master. release/0.33.x takes patch-rated fixes only (§ R2). § R2
    D3 The spec phase's evidence is formal models, executable reference models with property tests, spikes and live probes. No production kernel or driver code is written in it (§ R2's freeze). every flow
    D4 Each operation is designed in an unprimed session by the method of the research's § 4 (S1 to S5). § 9 step 3 builds the tool
    D5 The read() slice is the first flow, end to end; key, error, lifecycle and consistency layers grow from what read() needs. § 9 step 5
    D6 A spec defines abstract axes. Beside it, one profile table per axis records Memory, Local, S3 and SFTP, each cell citing a dated probe or a vendor document. every flow
    D7 A spec 001 to 050 gets a header marking it as 0.33 behaviour being superseded only when a new spec replaces sections of it. The new spec names each section it supersedes. A file left with no live section is deleted, and the ground register (D11) maps its old IDs to the new. the flow that supersedes
    D8 After the decisions, a separate pass compares a flow's spec with 0.33. The conformance suite on release/0.33.x is the evidence of what 0.33 does, not master's, which carries changes made after the tag (§ R2); a difference becomes a break record or a 0.33 defect, and the pass never changes a decision. No test cell is removed until a new one replaces it. step 4 of each flow
    D9 One 1.0.0 cut. Master publishes nothing until v1 is implemented; every release before it is a 0.33 patch from release/0.33.x. § R2; CONTRIBUTING.md § Release
    D10 OPEN, postponed by the maintainer: when kernel and driver work resumes. It is decided with the read() flow's evidence in hand (research § 9, Not yet). Until then § R2's pause holds. an RFC amending this one
    D11 One file, the ground register, lists every ground artifact, the flow that admitted it and its status (active or reopened). The design tool's hook reads its allowlist from it. § 9 step 3 creates it

    The flow and the ground are as the research's § 3 states them, and bind as stated there: design, record, spec, compare; the ground grows only from the new layer; 0.33's code, tests and specs 001 to 050 are never ground; a reopen is recorded and runs its own flow, and nothing changes ground on the side. At the start the ground holds prior art and spec 051 with its formal models. Two key artifacts are not ground: PR #1173's key model, until the key spec is accepted (open point 8), and spec 054 of open PR #1171, which was designed against spec 004 and probes of 0.33 backends. § 9 step 4 decides whether it is input to the key spec.

    R2. Step 1: the 0.33 line and the pause¶

    The branch. release/0.33.x was created on 2026-10-23 at the v0.33.0 tag's commit a51ac1b and pushed with no commits of its own. It admits what CONTRIBUTING.md § When to bump rates patch or no bump: bug fixes, patch-eligible floor raises, CI, docs and metadata. A change rated minor is refused there.

    Master first. A 0.33 fix merges to master and is cherry-picked to the branch, so that it ships in 1.0.0 as well; merging it forward from the branch would meet a master that has diverged since the tag. The branch's suite, not master's, is the 0.33 record D8 reads: master's carries the changes made after v0.33.0, the breaks among them listed in the migration guide's ## v0.33.0 to v1.0.0 section, and runs Memory on the kernel. Where the fixed code exists only on one line, the fix is ported rather than cherry-picked: Memory's code is hand-written at the tag and on the kernel on master. A fix that would break a caller, which the bump table rates minor, lands on master only if a clause of a spec 001 to 050 already states the fixed behaviour, so the code is wrong under Rule 3. Otherwise the fix decides behaviour no clause states, and it goes to that operation's flow (§ R3, class F).

    The pause (open point 5). RFC-0017 D3 steps 2 to 8 are paused, the retirements inside steps 2 and 4 with them, and so is D8 step 4's re-audit, which runs after the last step. MemoryBackend and AsyncMemoryBackend stay on the kernel. No further class moves onto it until the specs for what it implements exist and D10 is decided. ADR-0042's decisions are neither withdrawn nor amended: the pause changes when they are carried out, which D3 owns. D10 decides whether they stand.

    The freeze. During the pause the kernel and both Memory drivers change for two reasons only: to keep the gates green, and to fix a defect that a released 0.33 version also has. Nothing is added to them. A defect found only in kernel-era code is recorded and parked with the work it belongs to.

    Not done by the cut. The branch carries v0.33.0's ci.yml and ci-full.yml, whose on: blocks name master only, so a pull request into it runs no CI until the first one into it adds the branch to both. Neither branch protection nor a patch release's checklist for the branch exists yet (Open Questions). No 0.33 patch is released before all three exist.

    R3. Open backlog items (open point 10)¶

    Every open item falls in one class. Items do not move to the branch: the backlog is master's, and a fix reaches the branch as its fix PR's cherry-pick or port (§ R2).

    Class What it holds Disposition
    K A defect in 0.33: code against its own spec or docs Stays open. Fixed master first; patch-rated fixes reach the branch.
    P Kernel, driver, Session or migration work: due at a D3 step, or building toward one Stays in its section; its entry reads "paused (RFC-0024)". Resumes when D10 is decided.
    F An open decision about what a caller observes, which no clause of a spec 001 to 050 decides Re-scoped: decided in its operation's flow, its options as candidate input, as research § 7 holds read()'s.
    S Rework of a spec 001 to 050 for its own sake Paused (D7). A K fix still amends the clause it fixes.
    U Process, tooling, CI, docs and extensions that do not depend on 0.33's behaviour Unchanged.

    The items this RFC disposes of are those whose index entry or dossier names an RFC-0017 D3 step, a kernel migration or a spec 001 to 050 rework. They were found by hatch run backlog-find "D3 placement|D3 step|step [2-8]" --dossiers --max 80 and a Grep of sdd/BACKLOG.md for kernel|D3|driver|Session, at 74538f0, and clustered in the trace (sdd/traces/bk-485-reset-rfc.yml, orient:), which carries the evidence per cluster. The other open items were not classified one by one; whoever works one classifies it by the table above first.

    R4. How a v1 spec relates to 0.33 code and to SPEC-DESIGN.md¶

    A spec in the ground binds code only once the flow implementing it begins. Until then, a difference between it and 0.33 is a break record or a 0.33 defect (D8), not a defect in either the code or the spec. A ground clause that a binding spec incorporates by reference binds through that reference, as far as that spec binds: spec 053's BE-032 states the store tree as spec 051's TREE clauses, so a kernel or Memory answer contradicting one of them is a K defect. Both sentences are added to 000-process.md Rule 3. Rule 5's "not removed" gains D7's one exception: a file D7 deletes maps its IDs to their successors in the ground register. No ID changes meaning.

    SPEC-DESIGN.md binds every v1 spec, with one exception. The read() slice, § 9 step 5, is written in the provisional F1 to F6 form as the format experiment that step 6 measures. The read() spec names, in its Notes, each SPEC-DESIGN.md rule its form departs from. The format RFC then adopts the form or the slice is rewritten. No other spec takes the provisional form before that RFC.

    R5. Break records (open point 9)¶

    One register, sdd/v1-breaks.md, holds every break that is decided and not yet implemented; the first comparison pass creates it. An implemented break's record is the migration guide's ## v0.33.0 to v1.0.0 section, which already holds the breaks master made after v0.33.0 and takes a breaking K fix (§ R2) directly. A row per break:

    Field Holds
    ID BRK-NNN, numbered in order and never reused
    v1 clause the section ID of the v1 spec that differs
    0.33 behaviour what 0.33 does, with its evidence: a conformance test's node ID or a spec 001 to 050 section ID
    Caller who is affected and what they change
    Status decided, implemented or withdrawn
    Guide once implemented, the migration-guide anchor of its section

    D8's pass adds a row for each difference that is not a 0.33 defect; one that is a 0.33 defect becomes a K item instead. A break reaches the migration guide when it becomes real, not when it is decided: the PR that implements it writes its subsection under ## v0.33.0 to v1.0.0 and its **Breaking** CHANGELOG entry under the existing rule (CONTRIBUTING.md § Release, Phase 1, the migration-guide line), and sets the row's Status and Guide. The guide is published from master on every push (docs.yml deploy-dev), so a break written into it before its code would describe behaviour no version has. Before the 1.0.0 cut, every row is implemented or withdrawn, and the guide's section is then the whole list of breaks the cut ships.

    Alternatives Considered¶

    The alternatives to D1 to D11 are the research's § 2 rows. Those of this session, all decided in the log above:

    • Scope: an RFC that edits only BK-485, leaving every D3 item's due date contradicting the pause until a later sweep; or a sweep of every open item, for a much larger PR whose U-class edits change nothing.
    • Fix flow: fixes on the branch first and merged forward into a master that has diverged since the tag; or a fixable kernel during the pause, which is the production kernel code D3 rules out.
    • The 0.33 record (fix pass, PR #1177): the v0.33.0 tag's suite, from which every patch fix must be subtracted by hand; or master's suite less the migration guide's deltas, which list caller-visible breaks rather than every changed cell.
    • Spec 051 under Rule 3 (fix pass): exempted by name, which leaves the next ground spec an old spec cites undecided.
    • A 0.33 defect also in the kernel: fixed on the branch only, against the master-first rule; or fixed nowhere, so the 0.33 line ships it.
    • Fixes only: bug and security fixes alone, freezing the 0.33 line's support matrix at v0.33.0; or support additions rated minor shipped as patches, which bends semver.
    • Status: Proposed, with acceptance and RFC-0017's note in a later PR; or an ADR beside this RFC for D1, D7, D8 and D9. The pause amends RFC-0017's D3, which ADR-0042 left to the RFC as process, so no ADR is amended.
    • Break records: each flow's research doc lists its own breaks and the guide is written from all of them at the cut, which leaves no list of decided breaks to check the cut against; or the guide grows as breaks are decided, publishing behaviour no version has. In the fix pass: a register of every break, backfilled with master's existing ones, which would give an implemented break two homes.
    • Authority: amending CLAUDE.md principle 5 as well, at a recorded process-budget raise; or leaving Rule 3 for step 3, so that until then this RFC and Rule 3 disagree on paper.
    • PR #1171: merged first, so that the key spec refines spec 054 and brings 0.33-grounded clauses into the ground against S1; or left unmentioned until step 4.
